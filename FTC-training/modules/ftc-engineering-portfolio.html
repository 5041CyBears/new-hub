Module Goals

By the end, students should be able to:

  • Treat the engineering portfolio as evidence of the team's engineering process, growth, decisions, and impact rather than a scrapbook.
  • Capture design iterations, quantitative analysis, tradeoffs, failures, tests, and implementation lessons throughout the season.
  • Organize content so judges can quickly understand the team and find evidence relevant to awards.
  • Write concise captions and explanations that connect actions to evidence and outcomes.
  • Use current FIRST award and portfolio requirements as the final authority each season.

Big Picture

The system at a glance

Engineering Story

The portfolio should show how the team identifies problems, develops alternatives, tests ideas, learns, and makes decisions—not just what the final robot looks like.

Evidence

Strong evidence includes sketches, CAD views, calculations, test tables, photos, graphs, decision matrices, code architecture, failure analysis, and measured outcomes.

Tradeoffs

Judges should be able to see why one option was chosen over another. Compare performance, complexity, cost, weight, risk, serviceability, and schedule.

Iteration

Document version A, what failed, what the test showed, and why version B changed. Failure is valuable evidence when it drives learning.

Team Story

The portfolio can also communicate team structure, outreach, sustainability, learning, and other award-relevant work when allowed/currently appropriate.

Findability

A portfolio is read under time pressure. Use clear sections, headings, captions, labels, and visual hierarchy so evidence can be found quickly.

Core Knowledge

Important concepts

Think Award

Current FTC award guidance continues to emphasize the engineering portfolio for the Think Award. FIRST's award summary identifies engineering-process evidence, implemented lessons, tradeoff/cost-benefit analysis, and mathematical analysis as valuable examples.

Capture During Season

Do not reconstruct months of engineering the night before submission. Build a habit of saving photos, sketches, test results, and decisions every week.

Caption With Meaning

A caption should explain what the reader is seeing and why it matters. 'Intake V3' is weaker than 'V3 widened the funnel 28 mm after off-center pickup testing reduced jams.'

Numbers Need Context

A graph or calculation is useful only if the reader knows the variable, units, test conditions, and what decision came from it.

Source of Truth

Portfolio format, page/file limits, submission method, and award criteria can change. Verify the current competition manual/event resources rather than copying last year's checklist.

Student Voice

The strongest portfolio sounds like students understand the engineering. Students should be able to explain any major claim, chart, or decision included.

Practical Advice

What students should actually do

  • Assign a rotating documentation role during build/testing so evidence collection is everyone's job.
  • Create a shared folder structure by subsystem and date so photos and data are findable later.
  • After a design review, save the options considered, criteria, decision, and unresolved risks.
  • Use before/after comparisons and test tables instead of long paragraphs when they communicate more clearly.
  • Write captions that answer: What? Why? Evidence? Result?
  • Audit the portfolio against current award criteria without turning it into a keyword dump.
  • Have students who did not write a section read it; if they cannot understand the decision, improve it.
  • Keep raw source evidence outside the final portfolio so judges' questions can be answered accurately.

Competition Habits

Make the system repeatable and recoverable

Make state visible

Use labels, telemetry, logs, marks, checklists, or diagrams so students can tell what is happening without guessing.

Test failure modes

Create controlled bad conditions and practice safe recovery instead of testing only perfect operation.

Train backups

More than one student should know how to operate, explain, troubleshoot, and repair the system.
