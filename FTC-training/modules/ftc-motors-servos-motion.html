Module Goals

By the end, students should be able to:

  • Choose between DC motors, positional servos, and continuous-rotation servos for common FTC mechanisms.
  • Explain the tradeoff between speed and torque through gearing.
  • Use encoder information appropriately for position and velocity control.
  • Estimate output speed and ideal torque multiplication through a gear ratio.
  • Recognize stalls, overheating, shock loads, and poor mechanical advantage before they become failures.

Big Picture

The system at a glance

DC Gearmotors

FTC DC motors are well suited to continuous rotation and higher-power mechanisms such as drivetrains, intakes, lifts, and arms. The gearbox determines much of the speed/torque behavior.

Positional Servos

A standard servo is useful when a mechanism needs a limited angular position: claws, latches, wrists, guides, or selectors. Design the linkage so the servo does not fight a hard stop.

Continuous-Rotation Servos

CR servos behave more like small speed-controlled motors. They are useful for light rollers or simple motion, but they do not inherently command a repeatable angle.

Encoders

Motor encoders give measured motion. They support position, velocity, odometry, homing logic, and closed-loop control, but only if the mechanical connection and software interpretation are correct.

Gearing

Reduction trades speed for torque. A larger driven gear/sprocket/pulley relative to the driver usually reduces speed and multiplies available torque.

Mechanical Advantage

Do not solve every load problem with a larger motor. Better leverage, counterbalance, smaller spool radius, lower friction, or a better gear ratio can reduce required actuator load.

Core Knowledge

Important concepts

Stall Is a Design Condition

A motor at stall draws high current while not turning. Mechanisms that regularly hit hard stops or bind should be redesigned and protected rather than treated as normal operation.

Speed Under Load

Free speed is not working speed. Real mechanisms slow under load, and voltage drop, friction, wheel compression, and gearing affect the result.

Servo Geometry

A linkage can amplify or reduce motion and force. Place the servo horn and link attachment points intentionally instead of selecting angles by trial and error only.

Hard Stops

Use software and physical limits thoughtfully. A servo or motor continuously driving into a hard stop wastes current and heats components.

Backdriving

Some mechanisms can fall or move when disabled. Consider gravity, gearbox backdrivability, counterbalance, brake strategies, and safe service position.

Shock Loads

Game-piece impacts and abrupt reversals can load gearboxes and shafts far above smooth-running conditions. Add compliance, ramping, robust support, or another protective design feature.

Practical Advice

What students should actually do

  • Start by writing required output speed, required travel, estimated load, and duty cycle before picking an actuator.
  • Use gear ratios to put the motor in a useful operating range instead of running near stall.
  • Measure actual mechanism speed with a real game piece; do not rely only on motor free-speed calculations.
  • Design servo linkages in CAD or sketch geometry so the desired mechanism travel fits within safe servo motion.
  • Use mechanical stops to protect the mechanism, but do not command an actuator to push continuously into them.
  • Track motor and servo temperature during repeated-cycle tests.
  • Where gravity can move the mechanism when disabled, establish a safe maintenance procedure.
  • Keep spare horns, adapters, gearboxes, and known-good actuators for high-use mechanisms.

Competition Habits

Make the system repeatable and recoverable

Make state visible

Use labels, telemetry, logs, marks, checklists, or diagrams so students can tell what is happening without guessing.

Test failure modes

Create controlled bad conditions and practice safe recovery instead of testing only perfect operation.

Train backups

More than one student should know how to operate, explain, troubleshoot, and repair the system.
