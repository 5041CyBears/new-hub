Module Goals

By the end, students should be able to:

  • Explain the roles of camera hardware, coprocessor software, PhotonVision/Limelight-style pipelines, robot code, and field geometry.
  • Distinguish 2D target yaw/pitch aiming from full 3D AprilTag pose estimation.
  • Calibrate cameras correctly and define a measured robot-to-camera transform.
  • Account for latency and timestamps when fusing vision with odometry/pose estimators.
  • Evaluate tag count, distance, ambiguity, motion blur, exposure, and camera placement before trusting a measurement.
  • Build targeting logic that handles no-target states, robot motion, interpolation, and logging instead of blindly steering at a pixel.

What Vision Can Do

Choose the simplest measurement that solves the problem

2D aiming

Yaw/pitch/area can be enough to center on a visible target or estimate simple range.

3D tag tracking

Camera-to-tag transforms enable field-relative robot pose estimates when calibration and geometry are correct.

Object detection

Can find game pieces or custom objects, but often has different accuracy/latency requirements than AprilTags.

Calibration

3D vision starts with camera intrinsics

PhotonVision requires calibration at the desired resolution for accurate AprilTag 3D tracking. Calibration belongs to the physical camera/resolution—not just the camera model name.
PhotonVision: PhotonVision recommends ChArUco-style calibration images across varied positions/angles and warns inaccurate calibration causes poor 3D performance. Open documentation
Exposure & Motion Blur

A crisp lower-exposure image can be more valuable than a bright blurry image

Exposure

Long exposure brightens the image but increases motion blur on a moving robot.

Gain / lighting

Use lighting/gain to recover visibility after reducing exposure, while watching noise and flicker.

PhotonVision: PhotonVision’s current quick-configure guidance specifically recommends lowering exposure to reduce AprilTag motion blur. Open documentation

Extrinsics

Robot-to-camera transform must match the real mounting

Measure camera position and rotation relative to the robot coordinate frame. A 2 cm or several-degree mounting error becomes a systematic pose/targeting error—especially at distance.

AprilTags & Ambiguity

One image can sometimes fit more than one plausible 3D tag pose

PhotonVision exposes pose ambiguity and recommends multi-tag approaches where possible. Its 3D tracking guidance suggests rejecting single-tag poses with ambiguity ratio above 0.2 as one mitigation strategy.

Latency & Timestamps

The image describes where the robot was—not necessarily where it is now

For pose fusion, use the measurement timestamp associated with image capture/processing. Applying an old pose as though it were “now” creates errors while the robot moves.

Pose Fusion

Vision and odometry have different strengths

Wheel/gyro odometry

High-rate and smooth, but accumulates drift from slip and model error.

Vision

Can anchor to field geometry, but is slower, intermittent, and can produce outliers.

A pose estimator should fuse trusted timestamped vision measurements with odometry rather than replacing the robot pose with every camera result.

Practical Advice

Vision fails when teams skip calibration, geometry, timestamps, or evidence

  • Rigidly mount the camera. A camera that moves relative to the robot invalidates your extrinsic transform.
  • Test under motion, not just with the robot sitting on blocks.
  • Log every rejected measurement and the reason during development.
  • Use multiple tags when available and reduce trust at long range/high ambiguity rather than treating every result equally.
  • Keep targeting logic independent from vendor UI. The robot code should express target/pose goals, not depend on a person clicking the right dashboard tab.
  • Back up PhotonVision/Limelight configurations before events and label which physical camera goes with which calibration.
