Module Goals

By the end, students should be able to:

  • Identify the major FRC submitted awards and who each one recognizes.
  • Explain the 2027 FIRST Impact Award submission refresh and current interview format.
  • Turn outreach and team stories into specific, measurable evidence.
  • Plan an Impact presentation that fits the official time limit and leaves room for questions.
  • Prepare for FIRST Leadership Award and Woodie Flowers Award nominations with practical examples.
  • Use a repeatable team workflow for drafting, reviewing, practicing, submitting, and improving.
Submitted Awards

These awards are prepared before the event

FIRST Impact Award

Recognizes a team for sustained, measurable impact and embodiment of the FIRST mission.

FIRST Leadership Award

Recognizes outstanding individual student leadership, STEM growth, and long-term commitment to FIRST.

Woodie Flowers Award

Recognizes a mentor who inspires students through excellent communication, motivation, and integrity.

Important: Submitted awards are separate from normal pit judging. Treat the written submission and any scheduled interview as their own project with its own deadlines.

2027 Impact Award Refresh

The submission format changed significantly for 2027

Removed

  • Executive summary questions
  • Repeated short-form answers that duplicated the essay

Current 2027 format

  • Three essay questions
  • Up to 4,000 characters per question
  • All three questions are weighted equally
  • Updated definitions, including a definition for “sustained”
  • Revised feedback questions after events

Verified for the 2027 season: FIRST announced this refresh on August 28, 2026. The optional video, optional documentation form, and interview process remain in place.

Impact Award — Current Submission Questions

Think of the three essays as three different lenses

1

Spread the FIRST mission

Choose the outreach that best shows how your team brings FIRST to people who may not already know it.

2

Impact the FIRST community

Show how your team strengthens other FIRST teams, events, mentors, volunteers, or shared resources.

3

Inspire the STEM future and sustain the team

Connect student outcomes, STEM pathways, advocacy or curriculum work, and the systems that keep your team healthy long term.

Writing strategy: Use the last three years for context, then spend the most detail on the strongest work from roughly the last 12–18 months. FIRST specifically encourages measurable data.

Practical Writing Advice

Write for a judge who has never met your team

Do

  • Lead with the important result, then explain how you achieved it.
  • Use numbers only when they help a judge understand scale or change.
  • Define acronyms and use FIRST terms consistently.
  • Separate a program you started from one you merely attended.
  • Say who did the work, how often it happens, and whether it continues.
  • Keep a source spreadsheet so every claim can be verified later.

Avoid

  • Long lists of events with no explanation of why they mattered.
  • Reusing the same accomplishment in all three essays unless the connection is genuinely different.
  • Inflated language such as “changed the entire community” without evidence.
  • Counting people in ways the team cannot explain or reproduce.
  • Assuming judges know local programs, school names, or team history.
  • Waiting until build season to reconstruct a year of outreach data.

Impact Documentation

Treat documentation as your team’s evidence system

Plan

Record the goal, audience, student leads, partners, schedule, and intended outcome before an initiative starts.

Capture

Track dates, participation, repeat engagement, photos, partner confirmation, and student reflection while the work is happening.

Evaluate

Ask what changed, what should be improved, and whether the program should continue, expand, or stop.

The official Impact Documentation Form is optional but strongly encouraged. FIRST recommends finalizing the linked file about a week before each event so judges have time to review it.

Optional Impact Video

The video supports the story — it does not replace the submission

Current official basics

  • Optional submission
  • 1–3 minutes
  • 16:9 format
  • Publicly downloadable link on an approved hosting service
  • Clean audio and no copyright problems
  • Closed captions are strongly encouraged

Practical production advice

  • Show students doing the work rather than narrating over logos for three minutes.
  • Use one clear message and a few strong examples.
  • Test the submitted link in an incognito/private window.
  • If you show it in the interview, bring the equipment and count it inside your presentation time.
  • Do not depend on venue Wi-Fi.

Important: unless the team chooses to show the video during the presentation, FIRST states that the video is not judged as part of the submission.

Impact Interview

You have 12 minutes total

Up to 7:00Student presentation + setup
Remaining timeJudge-led Q&A, normally about 5 minutes if presentation uses all 7

Official constraints

  • No more than three pre-college students present or answer questions.
  • One adult mentor may attend as a silent observer.
  • Setup time is part of the presentation time.
  • Interview recording or photography is not allowed.

5041 practice target

  • Rehearse the planned presentation around 6:00–6:20.
  • Leave margin for walking in, placing materials, and recovering from a stumble.
  • Practice both with and without slides/video.
  • Every presenter should be ready to answer beyond their scripted section.

Presentation Construction

Make the presentation memorable, not encyclopedic

1. Hook

Start with the one idea you want judges to remember after interviewing every team.

2. Three Pillars

Organize the middle around the three current essay lenses instead of reciting a year-by-year activity list.

3. Proof

Attach specific numbers, outcomes, partners, or student stories to each pillar.

4. Students

Make clear what students designed, led, learned, or changed.

5. Sustainability

Show how programs and the team survive leadership turnover, graduation, and changing resources.

6. Close

End with a short statement that connects your work back to FIRST’s mission and your team identity.

Q&A Advice

Three students should sound like one team

Strong habits

  • Pause for a beat before answering.
  • Hand off intentionally: “Jordan led that project and can give the exact numbers.”
  • Correct each other politely if a number is wrong.
  • Say “I don’t know” rather than inventing a fact.
  • Use the judge’s wording in the first sentence of your answer.

Common problems

  • One presenter answers everything.
  • Students repeat the scripted presentation instead of answering the question.
  • Presenters use statistics they cannot explain.
  • Students speak about outreach they never participated in and cannot describe.
  • Students compete with each other for speaking time.
FIRST Leadership Award

Nominate a student for individual leadership — not just technical skill

Eligibility

Teams may nominate up to two students in 10th or 11th grade who actively participate as team members.

What matters

Leadership, FIRST Core Values, awareness of FIRST, long-term commitment, individual team contribution, STEM experience, and ability to motivate others.

2027 deadline

February 4, 2027 at 3:00 PM ET. The nomination is submitted by an eligible adult submitter or Lead Coach.

Practical test: if you removed the student’s name from the essay, would the evidence still clearly describe one unusually strong individual — or could it describe any good team member?

Leadership Interview Preparation

The nomination earns the conversation — the student must own the examples

Before the event

  • Read the submitted nomination carefully.
  • Prepare examples for every claim the mentor made.
  • Identify two leadership failures or challenges and what changed afterward.
  • Be ready to separate “what the team did” from “what I personally contributed.”
  • Practice concise answers about future involvement in FIRST.

During the interview

  • Treat it as a conversation, not a memorized speech.
  • Use first-person evidence without sounding boastful.
  • Credit teammates and mentors while still explaining your own role.
  • Use specific technical examples when discussing STEM mastery.
  • Show reflection: strong leaders can explain what they would do differently.
Woodie Flowers Award

The essay is about how a mentor inspires through communication

Student-led

Students choose the mentor and students write the nomination in their own words.

3,000 characters

The essay must be concise enough to show the student writers can communicate clearly.

2027 deadline

February 4, 2027 at 3:00 PM ET. A designated student award submitter completes the nomination.

Core question: do not write a résumé of everything the mentor has ever done. Show how the mentor’s communication changed how students think, solve problems, work together, or explain engineering.

Submission Mechanics

Small process mistakes can erase a lot of good writing

Before the deadline

  • Write in a shared document first; paste into the portal only after editing.
  • Keep a plain-text backup of every answer.
  • Do not use < or > characters in award submissions because FIRST warns they can prevent saving.
  • Submit before the final hour so account or portal problems can be solved.
  • Save the confirmation email or screenshot.

AI use

  • FIRST permits AI assistance for award submissions.
  • Teams must provide proper credit/attribution when AI is used.
  • Students still need to understand and verify every claim.
  • Do not let a writing tool invent participation numbers, partners, dates, or outcomes.
  • The final voice should still sound like the team that judges will meet.

2027 Impact Recognition Update

Impact will be celebrated earlier at events

What changed

For 2027, FIRST announced that the FIRST Impact Award will be the first team award presented, at the start of playoffs.

What did not change

This ceremony-order update does not change the submission criteria, written review, or Impact interview format.

FIRST also announced plans to recognize teams that submit for the Impact Award during event ceremonies. Treat that recognition as celebration of the work — not as evidence that judging has already favored a team.
