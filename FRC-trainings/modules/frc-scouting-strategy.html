Module Goals

By the end, students should be able to:

  • Design a scouting system around decisions: match planning, ranking strategy, playoff alliance selection, and partner communication.
  • Collect and validate autonomous, teleop, endgame, defense, reliability, penalty, and role-fit data consistently.
  • Use official event data and community analytics as supporting context without confusing estimates with observed team scouting.
  • Build pick-list and match-plan processes that combine quantitative data, qualitative observation, current robot condition, and strategic fit.
  • Communicate recommendations clearly to drive team and alliance partners under time pressure.

Big Picture

The system at a glance

Match Scouting

Track observable robot actions by phase: auto, teleop cycles, scoring locations, misses, defense, endgame, failures, penalties, and recovery. Definitions must be consistent across scouts.

Pit / Robot Context

Pit conversations can confirm capabilities, auto paths, mechanism changes, repair status, preferred roles, and dimensions—but observed match performance still matters.

Official Data

FRC Events is the official source for event schedule/results/rankings. The Blue Alliance provides a widely used interface/API, while Statbotics provides predictive/derived analytics such as EPA.

Capability vs. Contribution

A robot's maximum demonstrated capability is different from its average match output. Alliance strategy needs both plus reliability and current trend.

Pick Lists

A playoff pick list is not simply the ranking table. Evaluate scoring complementarity, auto compatibility, defense, reliability, endgame, penalties, robustness, and strategic flexibility.

Match Plans

Translate scouting into actions: auto starting locations, traffic lanes, cycle allocation, defense target, protected zones, endgame timing, and communication triggers.

Core Knowledge

Important concepts

Metric Definitions

Define exactly what scouts click. A 'cycle' should have a start/end definition; a 'miss' should not depend on personal judgment.

Derived Metrics

EPA/OPR-like estimates can be useful context but are models, not direct measurement of a robot acting alone. Understand assumptions and do not double-count them with raw events.

Reliability Trend

Track non-starts, disconnects, brownouts, mechanism failures, degraded operation, and whether a repair appears successful.

Defense Context

Defense changes both the defended robot and defender's scoring opportunity. Note who defended whom and observable delay/disruption rather than only assigning a subjective score.

Auto Compatibility

Two excellent autonomous routines can still collide. Record starting position, path, field zones, timing, and fallback options.

Strategy Meeting

Use a defined agenda and remove team-number bias: data quality → role needs → reliability → candidate comparison → observation/video review → final ordered list.

Practical Advice

What students should actually do

  • Design the scouting form backward from the decisions drive team and alliance-selection group actually need.
  • Train scouts with recorded matches and compare results until definitions are consistent.
  • Use validation rules for impossible values and spot-check totals against official match outcomes.
  • Keep notes on robot changes; a team can become substantially better or worse during an event.
  • Build auto-path maps, not just 'has auto' checkboxes.
  • Separate raw observed events from calculated metrics in the database/UI.
  • During alliance selection, anonymize or sort by data first to reduce reputation/team-number bias before discussion.
  • Make pre-match briefs short enough for drive team to use: roles, risks, auto conflicts, defense priority, and endgame plan.

Competition Habits

Make the system repeatable and recoverable

Make state visible

Use labels, telemetry, logs, marks, checklists, or diagrams so students can tell what is happening without guessing.

Test failure modes

Create controlled bad conditions and practice safe recovery instead of testing only perfect operation.

Train backups

More than one student should know how to operate, explain, troubleshoot, and repair the system.
